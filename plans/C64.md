# C64 — the Commodore 64 in the Open Micro Emulator

Binding under `AGENTS.md` and under `plans/BEEB.md`, whose decisions (§0) —
the hostile guest, ROMs authored in Rust, the clean-room boundary,
cycle-exact timing, the audio clock pacing the machine, determinism, names,
one library — bind this model unchanged. This plan adds the Commodore 64 as
a further model of `tairix-openmicro`: the machine, the 1541 drive it talks
to over the serial bus, their media, and four clean-room ROMs written for
TAIRiX — a KERNAL, a BASIC equivalent to BASIC V2, a character ROM and a
1541 DOS.

Read first: `plans/BEEB.md` §0 (decisions), §1 (crate shape), §4 (the
worker), §5 (the front end) and §7 (verification).

## 0. Binding decisions

1. **The machine.** A Commodore 64 with 64 KiB of RAM, PAL (the 6569 at
   985 248 Hz, 312 lines of 63 cycles) or NTSC (the 6567R8 at
   1 022 727 Hz, 263 lines of 65 cycles), chosen per machine; a 6581 or an
   8580 SID; one or two 1541 drives as devices 8 and 9; the datasette; two
   joystick ports; the expansion port. Not emulated: the REU and other RAM
   expansions, the 1571 and 1581, the C128 and SX-64, and devices on the
   user port, which a program finds absent.
2. **The drive is a true 1541.** Its own 6502 at 1 MHz, 2 KiB of RAM, two
   6522 VIAs and a GCR head over a rotating surface, running the clean-room
   DOS by default; fast loaders and copy protection see real hardware. A
   user may install their own DOS image, as any ROM (BEEB §0.6).
3. **Inputs are the published record.** The Commodore 64 Programmer's
   Reference Guide, the Commodore 64 User's Guide, the 1541 User's Guide,
   the 6510, 6526, 6567/6569, 6581 and 6522 data sheets, published hardware
   analyses read as prose, the chips' behaviour as emulators licensed
   GPL-2.0-or-later record it (VICE, reSID, libresidfp — read for facts,
   never transcribed), and genuine ROMs observed as opaque programs.
   No ROM listing or disassembly, annotated or not, is read; genuine ROM
   bytes never enter the tree, a fixture or a build (BEEB §0.5).
4. **Compatibility is with the documented machine.** The KERNAL's jump
   table and RAM vectors, the documented zero-page and page 2–3 workspace,
   BASIC's tokens, vectors, messages, workspace and five-byte floating-point
   format, the character set's codes and its functional graphics, and the
   DOS's command channel, job queue, buffers and error messages are
   reproduced at their documented places. An address inside an original
   ROM that software is found calling is kept, doing what the original's
   code there is observed to do (BEEB §0.4).
5. **Names.** The model is the *Commodore 64*, its drive the *1541*. The
   ROMs call themselves OpenMicro 64: BASIC's banner is
   `**** OPENMICRO 64 BASIC V2 ****`, the DOS's power-on message
   `73,OPENMICRO DOS 1541,00,00`; no ROM claims to be Commodore's.
6. **Two clocks, one time.** The machine counts its own cycles; the drive
   counts 1 MHz cycles. Drive time is the machine's scaled by the exact
   ratio of the two clocks, so neither drifts, and the drive is advanced to
   the moment of every serial-bus access either side makes.
7. **One canvas.** The Acorn models' 896 × 600 canvas holds the picture: a
   VIC-II pixel is two slots and a raster line two rows, so the 4:3 picture
   fits as theirs does. A C64 slot is a palette index; the model's palette
   expands it.

## 1. The machine

**Clock and bus.** One `u64` count of the machine's cycles. Each cycle has
a VIC-II half (φ1) and a CPU half (φ2); the VIC-II takes the CPU's half too
on bad lines and for sprite data, holding BA low three cycles before. The
CPU's reads stall while BA is low and RDY with it; its writes proceed, so a
stall begins at the instruction's first read after BA falls. The CPU's
reads stall in the bus, as the Model B's 1 MHz accesses stretch, so the CPU
core is unchanged. A stalled read is made once, when BA rises: the cycles it
waits reach no chip, so a register a read changes — an ICR, a collision
register — changes once (VICE reads so, and *Shadow of the Beast*'s NMI
chain, whose ICR read meets a bad line's BA, holds only so). While it waits
the bus carries the memory byte it addresses, the floating bus where a chip
or a cartridge would answer, which a bad line's first three cycles take as
their colour.

**The 6510 port and the PLA.** `$00` and `$01` are the port's direction and
data. LORAM, HIRAM and CHAREN with the expansion port's GAME and EXROM pick
one of the documented memory configurations for the CPU; the VIC-II sees
its 16 KiB bank, chosen by CIA2's port A, with the character ROM at
`$1000–$1FFF` of banks 0 and 2. Port bits set as inputs read their pull-ups,
and bits 6 and 7, unconnected, keep the last value driven until it fades.
The datasette's write, sense and motor lines are bits 3, 4 and 5; the motor
line has no pull-up, and the motor switch's base resistor pulls it low, so
an undriven motor line runs the motor (the service manual's schematic). The
deck follows the port from power-on, through a reset and on every write.

**Colour RAM.** 1 K × 4 bits at `$D800`; the upper nibble reads as the
floating bus: the byte the VIC-II read in the cycle's first half, which is
also what an unmapped read sees and what a write to `$00`/`$01` leaves in
the RAM beneath the port.

**The SO input.** The CPU core gains the 6502's SO pin, whose falling edge
sets V; the 1541's byte-ready line drives it.

**Board revisions.** The SID chosen fixes the CIAs: the 6581's boards carry
the 6526, the 8580's the 8521.

**Bus monitors.** While asked, the machine records the CPU's accesses to the
chips and the expansion port's I/O, colour RAM aside, and each drive its
CPU's accesses to its VIAs, each at its own cycle, as BEEB §2.1's monitor
does, for the reference harness and the debugger. A read the VIC-II stalls
is recorded once, when it is made.

## 2. Devices

**CIAs.** Two 6526 (or 8521) at `$DC00` and `$DD00`: timers A and B in each
of their modes, TOD counting the mains rate the machine's standard implies
(50 or 60 Hz) with latch and alarm, the serial register, and the interrupt
control register with the chip's delay between a cause and its line. CIA1's
ports scan the keyboard matrix and read the joysticks; its interrupt is the
CPU's IRQ. CIA2's port A picks the VIC-II bank and drives the serial bus's
ATN, CLOCK and DATA; its interrupt is the CPU's NMI. RESTORE's press,
coupled through a capacitor, triggers a 556 that pulls NMI low for one
period, 1.1 × 47 kΩ × 360 pF (18.6 µs), however long the key is held; a
press within the period leaves it alone.

**VIC-II.** Stepped per cycle from the published timing: the refresh,
c-, g-, p- and s-accesses at their cycles, the display and idle states,
bad lines from YSCROLL, the sprite DMA with its BA timing, priorities and
both collision kinds, the border unit's two flip-flops, raster and light-pen
interrupts. Each cycle draws its eight pixels into the canvas at the beam's
position. The palette is computed from the chips' published luminance
levels and colour angles.

**SID.** Stepped at the machine's clock: three oscillators with their
waveforms, sync and ring modulation, the noise generator's register, the
combined waveforms (digitally ANDed, then each bit's pulldown on its
neighbours from libresidfp's fitted model); envelopes with their rate
counters, exponential decay, the ADSR delay bug and reSID 1.0's measured
pipeline delays; R-2R ladder DACs per chip (the 6581's arms 2.2R and
unterminated); the filter, a state-variable loop on each chip's measured
cutoff curve (the 6581's broken at `$400`, the 8580's near straight); the
6581's DC offsets, which let the volume register sound; the output stage's
16 kHz low pass and 16 Hz high pass; POTX and POTY from the paddles CIA1
switches in; OSC3 and ENV3; the data bus a write-only register reads,
fading. Its output, the mean of every four cycles, is resampled to 48 kHz
by `tairix_audio::resample`, the one resampler. A paddle moved at a cycle is
read from the first reading after it.

**Devices on threads of their own.** The SID's voices, filter and output
stage, and the drives, each run on a lane (`machine::lane`): a thread brings
the device up behind the machine to the cycle the machine says it has run to,
and the machine posts what it does to the device stamped with its cycle and
brings the device up itself wherever it needs it as it stands at a cycle — a
read of OSC3 or ENV3, of CIA2's port A, the end of a slice, a snapshot —
waiting on the thread for one piece of work at most. A device changes only by
its inputs and the cycles it is brought up to, and a thread takes it no
further than bringing it up to any later cycle would have, so which thread
did the work never shows and a machine with no threads to be had runs the
same without them. The SID's data bus and paddle readings, which no sound
depends on, stay with the machine, so reading them waits on nothing.

## 3. The 1541 and the serial bus

The serial bus is three open-collector lines, wired-AND between the machine
and every drive. A drive is its own board: the 6502 at 1 MHz, 2 KiB of RAM at
`$0000`, its DOS ROM at `$C000` (mirrored at `$8000`), VIA1 at `$1800` on the
bus with ATN answered in hardware, VIA2 at `$1C00` on the head: stepper,
motor, LED, density, write protect, SYNC, and the byte-ready line to the
CPU's SO. The VIA is BEEB's 6522, shared, ticking on the drive's own clock.
A drive runs behind the machine and is brought up to it at each of the
machine's accesses to CIA2 and at the end of every slice; two drives take
turns of 32 cycles, so each sees what the other does to the bus. The drives'
thread takes two drives only through whole turns, so where it brings them up
never moves a turn's end; a write to CIA2, and with two drives a read of it,
is posted to them at its cycle, and a read of port A brings them up there.

The surface is a GCR bit stream per half-track. The head is timed from the
drive's 16 MHz crystal: reading, UE7 divides it by 16 less the selected
zone, UF4 counts UE7's carries, and every fourth clocks a bit — a one at the
first after a flux reversal, which restarts both, zeros after — with noise
standing in for reversals in long flux-free stretches; ten ones are SYNC,
and BYTE READY follows a byte's eighth bit by at least ten crystal cycles,
on the next drive cycle. Writing, the same clock shifts the drive's byte out,
a track bit a cell from where writing began. D64 images (35 or 40 tracks,
with or without the error block) are encoded to GCR as a drive formats
them, sector errors included; G64 images carry the GCR stream as is, each
half-track one ring at one zone (a per-byte zone table is read as the
track's standard zone). A surface written since it was last saved is
decoded back to the image it came from — a D64 only where every sector
still decodes as far as its checksum (a write's end may spoil the padding
after it), else G64 — and written back as BEEB §2.1's discs are.

## 4. Media

PRG and T64 files load into memory as the KERNAL's LOAD would leave them:
the end-of-load pointer past the last byte, and, loaded at the start of
BASIC's text, BASIC's variables beginning there (a T64 entry runs no
further than the next entry's bytes, its stated end being often wrong).
TAP images (versions 0 and 1) are pulse lengths played to CIA1's FLAG while
the motor runs and PLAY is down, and recording, with RECORD down too,
writes the write line's pulses back over what they cover. CRT images bring
their chips and hardware type to the expansion port: the generic 8K, 16K
and Ultimax kinds, Simons' BASIC (its upper half in on any write to
`$DE00`, whatever the value, and out on a read), Ocean, Fun Play, Super
Games, Epyx FastLoad, System 3, Dinamic, Zaxxon and Magic Desk; in the Ultimax
configuration ROMH answers the VIC-II's top 4 KiB of each bank. Inserting
or removing a cartridge resets the machine, the CPU, port, CIAs, SID,
cartridge and drives starting again and the VIC-II running on. Every image
is untrusted input, decoded in the machine worker against fixed bounds.

## 5. The ROMs

Each is Rust driving `rom::asm::Asm`, emitted by
`cargo xtask openmicro --write-roms` into `firmware/` (`c64-character.rom`,
`c64-basic.rom`, `c64-kernal.rom`, `c1541-dos.rom`), committed and gated as
BEEB §0.3's are.

- **Character ROM** (4 KiB): both sets, upper case with graphics and
  lower case with upper case, each 128 glyphs and their reverses; the
  letters and digits are this ROM's own design, the line and block graphics
  the exact shapes their function fixes.
- **KERNAL** (8 KiB at `$E000`): reset and the cartridge check, the memory
  test and I/O set-up, the screen editor (40 × 25, logical lines, colour and
  control codes), the keyboard scan and its tables, the jiffy clock, the
  serial bus protocol, the tape format, RS-232 over CIA2's NMI, the logical
  file layer, the jump table at `$FF81–$FFF3` and the vectors at
  `$0314–$0333`.
- **BASIC V2** (8 KiB at `$A000`): the tokeniser, the interpreter with every
  statement and function, five-byte floating point to the same bits as the
  original's, strings and their collection, the vectors at `$0300–$030B`
  and the documented workspace. BASIC and the KERNAL are one assembly, as
  the originals are one program: BASIC's code runs on into the KERNAL ROM's
  free room, and the KERNAL's routines never depend on BASIC being mapped.
- **1541 DOS** (16 KiB): the command channel and error channel, the file
  types, the BAM and directory, the job queue and the controller that runs it
  against the head, and the memory, block and user commands fast loaders
  use.

### The 1541 DOS

Two processes share the drive's 6502, as the original's do. The interface
runs the bus and the commands in the main loop, ATN entering it through
VIA1's CA1; the controller runs the job queue from VIA2's timer A
interrupt. They meet only at the job queue: the interface writes a job and
its buffer's track and sector, the controller replaces the job with its
return code.

- **Workspace at its documented places.** The job queue at `$00–$05` for
  buffers 0–5, each buffer's track and sector at `$06–$11`, the disk's ID,
  and the five buffers at `$0300–$07FF`. Job codes: `$80` read, `$90`
  write, `$A0` verify, `$B0` seek, `$C0` bump, `$D0` jump to the buffer,
  `$E0` seek then jump; return codes `$01` OK, `$02` no header, `$03` no
  SYNC, `$04` no data block, `$05` data checksum, `$06` byte decoding,
  `$07` verify, `$08` write protect, `$09` header checksum, `$0B` ID
  mismatch; an empty drive has no SYNC.
- **The head.** A track is reached a half-track at a time, a header's
  track correcting where the head was thought to be. A sector's header is
  matched by its GCR as it arrives, the wanted header encoded beforehand,
  since the data block follows too soon to decode one first; only a
  header that differs is decoded, to tell another sector from another
  track, another disk's ID or a bad checksum. The data block is read
  whole as GCR, its first 256 bytes into its buffer and the rest into the
  overflow at the stack page's top, the stack kept below it, then decoded
  in place from its start. A write encodes the buffer in place from its
  end, then starts writing at the old block's SYNC, continuing it with its
  own before the block and a gap after, and decodes the buffer back. GCR's
  code table is `media::c1541::gcr`'s, one definition.
- **The bus.** ATN is polled, by the idle loop through VIA1's latched CA1
  edge and by the transfers between bytes, so a command is never cut
  short. A talker holds each bit's setup about 100 µs with CLOCK held and
  its data valid about 75 µs with CLOCK released, as the original does,
  so a controller the VIC-II stalls sees every edge. A byte counts as sent
  only once taken: the talker checks ATN through each bit and its
  acknowledgement, since a controller lets DATA go just before it asserts
  ATN. The first command under ATN waits up to a millisecond for the
  controller to take CLOCK, since CLOCK not yet taken is not CLOCK let go
  to send; a drive busy when ATN came finds it taken and let go already.
  After the turnaround the drive holds CLOCK 80 µs at least before it is
  ready to send, the bus's published minimum. The interface never branches
  on V, which BYTE READY sets through SO whenever the disk turns.
- **Buffers.** Four buffers serve files, direct access and the directory
  alike, given out from the highest; the BAM keeps buffer 4. A request
  reads directory sectors into a buffer it holds until it is done, as do
  the commands walking chains, so with three files open another OPEN, a
  listing's included, is NO CHANNEL, as the original's is.
- **Direct access.** A `#` channel's pointer is the offset of the byte it
  holds ready; once that byte goes the pointer moves on, past the
  buffer's last byte to offset 1. The block commands, the memory commands
  and the user commands are each found by a letter in a table, one
  dispatcher for all; an `M-R` reply goes through the error channel ahead
  of its message.
- **Channels.** A file is the channel buffer it holds, its state kept
  beside the buffer, and a channel names its file. Each file read holds the
  next byte to send, fetched a byte ahead, so a block's successor is read,
  or the listing's next directory sector put into lines, before the last
  byte goes; the listing is the LOAD channel's alone, its command kept
  apart from the command buffer later commands reuse.
- **Writing.** A file's block is linked and written once a byte needs its
  successor, found by the original's rule; a file off the SAVE channel
  writes the BAM each time it begins a track, from a copy in its own
  buffer, with every track a block was taken from since the BAM was last
  read or written whole recorded wholly used. The BAM in memory is never
  trusted after a command fails part-way: the disk is read again.
- **Relative files.** One is open at a time, through its channel's buffer,
  holding one data block written back when another is wanted, and one
  holding a side sector; a cursor of block and offset follows the record
  and position. An extension finds first that the disk has every block it
  takes. No append or copy treats one as a stream of bytes.
- **Format.** Code an execute job's buffer jumps to, as the original's is:
  the head bumped, then each track written whole from its headers encoded
  beforehand, each sector verified, three tries a track.
- **Messages.** The error channel reads `nn,MESSAGE,tt,ss` and CR, then
  `00, OK,00,00`; the power-on message is `73,OPENMICRO DOS 1541,00,00`.
- **Verification.** Each part is tested on the true drive with this
  tree's KERNAL and BASIC driving it, against the screens and disk images
  the original DOS, run as an opaque program in the same drive, leaves.

### Behaviour pinned by observation

Each with its test beside the ROM's sources.

- **The KERNAL's start.** RAMTAS tests a byte of each page where the
  original's tests every byte, so READY comes about two seconds sooner from
  power-on; the top of memory it finds is the same.
- **Entry points software calls.** `$E518` sets the screen up as CINT does:
  the VIC-II's registers and the channels, then the editor's and keyboard's
  settings, the text colour, the cursor's phase and a clear; `$E51B` does the
  same but for the VIC-II, and `$E536` takes the text colour from A; `$E716`
  puts A on the screen, leaving interrupts on whatever its caller had, as
  CHROUT to the screen does. CINT at `$FF5B` runs on into `$FF5E`, which
  finds the standard and starts the jiffy timer, keeping X and Y as IOINIT
  does; the IRQ's entry is `$FF48`; RESTOR's table of the vectors' defaults
  is `$FD30`, which software copies itself. The bodies the jump table leads
  to are kept where software calls them: the serial bus's (`$ED09` TALK,
  `$ED0C` LISTEN, `$EDB9` SECOND, `$EDC7` TKSA, `$EDDD` CIOUT, `$EDEF`
  UNTLK, `$EDFE` UNLSN, `$EE13` ACPTR), SCNKEY's `$EA87` and UDTIM's
  `$F69B`.
  BASIC's RUN start (`$A659`), the rewind (`$A68E`), NEWSTT (`$A7AE`), the
  string print from A and Y (`$AB1E`), FAC to a word (`$B7F7`), the
  unsigned print of A (high) and X (`$BDCD`) and the cold start's halves,
  BASIC's RAM (`$E3BF`, CHRGET's pointer left at `$EA60`) and its vectors
  (`$E453`), leave memory as the original's do, scratch registers and the
  stack page's dead bytes aside. A program reading the original ROMs' own
  code as data — a decruncher keyed on the KERNAL's byte at `$E015` — runs
  only with images of the original ROMs fitted.
- **The KERNAL's editor.** CHRIN from the keyboard returns the entered
  line and RETURN without printing the newline: the cursor is left a column
  past the line's end on its row, and the caller prints the newline. Input
  starts where it began only when RETURN is pressed on that row. A line
  grows a second row whenever the cursor passes its last column, rows below
  moving down while AUTODN is 0; the cursor's row follows only onto column 40.
  DEL at a line's start moves left; INST still inserts in insert mode and DEL
  still deletes in quote mode. A clear and a scrolled-in row take the text
  colour. With the output elsewhere than the screen, RETURN moves the cursor
  to the next line itself. A character at the column past a full line,
  where an entered line leaves the cursor, is written there and the line
  grown after it, as the original does from the keyboard; its CHROUT varies
  there with the row and on the top row reads outside its tables, which
  this ROM does not reproduce.
- **The keyboard.** The highest numbered key held is the one decoded; SHIFT
  with the Commodore key changes the character set and decodes nothing; CTRL
  picks its table whatever else is held. A held key repeats 19 jiffies after
  it went down and every 4 after, for the cursor keys, INST DEL and the space
  bar. With no key held the scan clears LSTX and LSTSHF and calls no KEYLOG,
  leaving the repeat's counters as they stand; CINT leaves DELAY at 10, a
  new key setting it to 16.
- **The tape.** Short, medium and long pulses of 380, 534 and 702 cycles;
  leaders of 27136 short pulses before a header, 5376 before data and 79
  between a block's copies; only a first copy ends in a long and a short
  pulse. A data file's last byte is followed by a zero, which flags the end
  with the byte before it. After FOUND, 704 jiffies or the Commodore key or
  the space bar, the motor stopped meanwhile. The prompts are control
  messages.
- **RS-232.** Every OPEN and CLOSE returns `$F0`, the top of memory in X
  and Y, whether the buffers moved or not; the receiver listens from CHKIN;
  ENABL is CIA2's interrupts as written, SET among them; BITNUM is the word
  and one; a frame's start bit is a whole bit.
- **The serial bus.** A controller holds ATN and CLOCK a millisecond before
  its first byte, and after ATN waits a quarter millisecond for devices it
  did not address to let DATA go, so a name or command nothing listens for
  is DEVICE NOT PRESENT; UNLISTEN leaves every line released. A read
  nothing sends returns the bus port as it stands. Each of the bus calls
  keeps X and Y. A LOAD of a file holding only its load address waits until
  STOP, as the original's does.
- **The original pair's race.** Asked to talk on a channel with no
  file, the original DOS lets CLOCK go 68 µs after the turnaround, and the
  original KERNAL, reading the port twice alike, misses it behind a bad
  line. This machine reproduces it, so a recording that meets it hangs
  there each time it runs; this DOS holds the acknowledge 80 µs, and this
  KERNAL's waits count passes and read no ICR.
- **BASIC's output and files.** While CHANNL is set an entered line prints
  no new line and INPUT asks with neither `? ` nor `?? `; READY and BREAK go
  to the output as it stands, an error brings it back to the screen and
  ends CMD, and so does the warm start. READY is CR LF `READY.` CR LF and a
  message opens with CR LF. KERNAL error `$F0` sets MEMSIZ to the top and
  clears all a CLR does but close the files, then runs on. CLR, RUN and an
  entered line close every file. ST is a signed byte; PEEK keeps LINNUM for
  POKE and WAIT; the tokeniser copies a space without searching. INPUT#
  passes over a first line a RETURN opens, so a file at its end holds it
  there, and a character read as the device timed out ends the line unkept.
  At the keyboard INPUT# is ILLEGAL DIRECT once its file is open and the
  comma after its number read, and GET# before either.
- **The DOS's names.** The character before a colon names the drive, `1`
  DRIVE NOT READY; a name ends at a comma, a comma first being its one
  character and parting its fields too; the first two fields after it are
  each a type (`S`, `P`, `U`, `L`) or a mode (`R`, `W`, `A`, `M`) by its
  first letter, a later replacing an earlier, others and later fields
  ignored, and LOAD asks for a program. A buffer is taken first, so with
  three files open any OPEN is NO CHANNEL. The first entry whose name
  matches is the file, whatever its kind: unclosed is WRITE FILE OPEN, a
  type other than asked (its low three bits) FILE TYPE MISMATCH, an empty
  name FILE NOT FOUND. `*` matches what remains, `?` any character but the
  padding, sixteen characters at most count. A name that ends where it
  starts is written as the character it ends at, then a whole field of
  padding a byte past the field, which a new entry's zeros cover; a comma
  first ends one so, and one at the command's end is a zero and the
  field's padding. LOAD's `*` reopens the last
  program loaded by its first block, kept through `I` and a disk change,
  else finds the first program. `$` on another channel reads the directory
  as a file from the BAM on, without first making the disk ready.
- **The listing.** Each line 32 bytes with the load address `$0401` before
  the first and the program's end after the last: blocks as the line's
  number, three spaces below 10 blocks, two below 100, else one, and four
  less that after the kind; the name's sixteen bytes in quotes, its first
  shifted space or quote the closing quote, other shifted spaces spaces;
  `*` for unclosed, the kind by its low four bits, `<` for locked. Past REL
  the original shows what follows its table of kinds; this ROM shows `???`.
  The header is the BAM's name and its five bytes from the ID; the free
  count every track's but the directory's. `$` or `$0` lists everything and
  `$:` nothing; patterns follow the colon or the `$`, comma-parted, an empty
  one counting unless last, five kept; a pattern without a wildcard lists
  only its first match, the fifth's wildcards unseen; `=` and `S`, `P`, `U`
  or `R` lists one kind for all, a comma after it SYNTAX ERROR.
- **The DOS's reads.** A block's successor is read before its last byte
  goes, so one that cannot be read loses that byte; a link off the disk's
  tracks is ILLEGAL TRACK OR SECTOR, a sector past the track's READ ERROR.
  A last block whose link names no data byte sends the block whole; a lone
  block holding one byte sends it, then the block from its link, the byte
  last again. A looping chain reads without end.
- **The DOS's writes.** A new file's first block is on the track nearest
  the directory's, below before above, at its lowest free sector; each next
  ten sectors on, one less past the track's end, to the first free from
  there; a full track moves on away from the directory, the disk's edge to
  the other side's nearest stepping as from sector 0, a second edge DISK
  FULL with the last full block unwritten. No first block anywhere is
  ILLEGAL TRACK OR SECTOR as error 67 at 36,1. A data channel writes the
  BAM before the new unclosed entry, the SAVE channel the entry alone, so a
  protected disk refuses them at 18,0 and 18,1; the entry closes with its
  length and the BAM is written after. An empty file holds a RETURN. A name
  with a wildcard is SYNTAX ERROR 33; `@` replaces the first file a name or
  pattern finds, FILE TYPE MISMATCH where it is of another kind or a
  pattern finds nothing, its entry marked `$20` with the new first block at
  bytes 28–29 while a data channel writes, the new chain taking it over and
  the old freed at the close. A command keeps five names, those past the
  fifth before an `=` uncounted. An append counts the blocks before the last,
  one at least, then every block it writes. `S` scratches what the names
  before any `=` match, freeing a closed file's blocks, an unclosed one's
  kept, a locked file left, the count as FILES SCRATCHED's track. `R` takes
  one name each side of `=`, `C` one before it and the first four after,
  joined in turn and of the first one's kind, each with no wildcard or
  SYNTAX ERROR 30. `I`, `V` and `C` give up every open file unwritten and
  read the BAM again; `V` frees all but the BAM, uses the directory's
  sectors and each closed file's chain, and deletes each unclosed entry.
- **The DOS's format.** Each sector a SYNC of five bytes, its header, nine
  bytes of gap, a SYNC and the data block, then a gap of 8 bytes on tracks
  1–17, 18 on 18–24, 11 on 25–30 and 8 beyond; the first sector's SYNC a
  byte longer and the last gap the rest of the revolution. Each data block
  is `$4B` then `$01`s, track 1's opening with `$00`. The ID is the two
  characters after the name's comma, whatever they are, a missing second
  zero; with no comma, or nothing after it, the format is quick and the disk
  keeps its ID. The BAM header's padding runs on from where the name's field
  ended. Every open file is given up first. No colon is SYNTAX ERROR; drive
  1, a protected disk and a full format that fails are READ ERROR at 0,0; a
  quick format of a protected disk is WRITE PROTECT ON at 18,0, of a blank
  one READ ERROR at 18,0.
- **The DOS's direct access.** `#` alone takes the highest free buffer;
  `#` and a number, its spaces skipped and its digits read, none being 0,
  takes that one; buffer 4 and a busy one are NO CHANNEL. A channel's
  first byte is its buffer's number, then the bytes from offset 2; writes
  start at 1. `U1` sends 0 to 255, `B-R` 1 to the offset byte 0 gives,
  each with EOI on its last and on from 1 after it; `B-P` points; `B-W`
  puts the offset of the last byte written in byte 0. A `B-` command's
  numbers start after its letter, a `U` command's after its first space or
  comma, either's after a colon; spaces and commas part them, anything
  else is SYNTAX ERROR 30, a missing one 0. No channel is NO CHANNEL, drive
  1 DRIVE NOT READY at the block, a block no disk has ILLEGAL TRACK OR
  SECTOR at it. `B-A` and `B-F` work on the BAM as it stands: a used block
  is NO BLOCK at the next free one up the disk, never wrapping, 0,0 for
  none; the block's track and that one are touched, and the BAM is
  written straight after with every touched track recorded wholly used,
  its error standing over NO BLOCK, DISK ID MISMATCH at 18,0 before the
  disk is first read. The block reads and writes make the disk ready
  first. `M-R` sends its count of bytes, one without, 256 for 0, then
  RETURN with EOI, then the message; `M-W` writes its count; `M-E`, `U3` to
  `U8` at `$0500` and on three bytes each, and `B-E` run code; `U9` and
  `UI` restart the drive but for `UI+` and `UI-`, which are OK; `UJ` and
  `U:` restart it.
- **The DOS's relative files.** `name,L,` and a length byte makes a file or
  opens one of that length, the byte after the comma read whatever it is;
  another length than an existing file's is RECORD NOT PRESENT, 0 any, a
  file of another kind FILE TYPE MISMATCH, and `,L` with no length on a new
  name FILE NOT FOUND; the name alone opens one. A second is NO CHANNEL. A
  new file's first data block is found as any file's, its side sector the
  next from it; the block holds empty records, each `$FF` then zeros, a last
  part of one marked too, its link 0 and the offset of the last whole
  record's last byte, so a length of 255 makes a file of none. The entry is
  closed at once with the side sector and the record length, and counts its
  data blocks and side sectors only at a close after the file changed. A
  side sector holds its link, its number, the record length, the six side
  sectors' blocks and 120 data blocks', the last's link sector the offset
  of its last pointer's last byte. `P`, the channel plus 96, the record low
  byte first and the position take the command buffer's bytes as they
  stand, a missing one being whatever is there, the RETURN after a PRINT#
  included; record and position 0 are the first, a position past the record
  OVERFLOW IN RECORD from its start, a record past the file RECORD NOT
  PRESENT, standing through what follows. A record reads from the position
  to its last byte not zero, EOI with it, an empty one its `$FF` and one of
  zeros its first, then the next record; past the file a RETURN with EOI and
  RECORD NOT PRESENT. A write fills the record from the position, the rest
  zeroed at EOI, the next PRINT# going to the next record, a longer one
  OVERFLOW IN RECORD, its excess dropped. One past the file extends it a
  block at a time, each the next from the last and holding its records
  empty, and writes them, the side sector and the BAM at once. A block
  opening a new side sector links to a spare, the next from it, unless its
  extension adds it alone; the side sector is the next after that, every
  side sector's table naming it, and the next block added is the spare, so
  a file ending there keeps it, uncounted and in no list. `S` frees both chains, and `C` of one is DIR
  ERROR at 2,2.
- **The DOS's divergences.** The bytes past a last block's end are zero,
  where the original leaves what its buffer held. The original finds a
  file's next block as soon as one fills, so a file of whole blocks leaves a
  block allocated past its end, and on a disk with none left loses its last
  block to DISK FULL; this DOS finds a block only for a byte that needs one.
  Where `V` fails part-way the original keeps its half-made BAM, which lets
  the next file overwrite one it had yet to reach; this DOS reads the disk's
  BAM again. A full format takes about 28 seconds, the original's about 74.
  The original reads a file through two buffers where it can and once a file
  opens keeps the BAM in buffer 3, so with files open `#` finds other
  buffers free than this DOS's. A block command naming drive 1 leaves its
  channel as it was, where the original's then sends nothing until a read on
  drive 0. The original's restart from `UJ` or `U:` misses an ATN the
  controller raises before it finishes, so a command straight after hangs
  the bus; this DOS answers it. A relative file holds two buffers, its
  channel's for one data block and one for its side sector, where the
  original's holds three, so reading a record that spans two blocks takes
  three block reads where the original's takes one; as many others stay
  free. A length of 0, or none after `L,`'s
  comma, on a new name is FILE NOT FOUND, where the original's hangs with
  track 17 marked wholly used. A read placed past a record's last byte not
  zero sends the rest of the record, EOI with its last, then the next
  record, where the original's runs on through the records after, EOI
  falling as it chances. A record further than the disk's free blocks could
  reach is FILE TOO LARGE before a block is taken, as the 1541 manual has
  it, where the original's hangs. `C` of a relative file leaves the disk as
  it was, where the original's leaves a half-made copy and its track marked
  wholly used. A file written past its end after a read there keeps RECORD
  NOT PRESENT standing through its close, where the original's error
  channel then reads a broken message. A one-block extension of a file
  ending on a spare takes the spare, where the original's takes the next
  free block, leaving the spare used and in no file and the new block's link
  sector from the spare's.
- **Reals.** A result keeps 40 bits, rounded half up to 32 when stored or
  pushed as an operator's left operand. A sum shifts the smaller operand's
  40 bits right, keeps the larger's rounding byte, and carries one more into
  that byte when the exponents and signs are equal; a result with no bit
  above its rounding byte is zero. A product walks the multiplier's five
  bytes from the rounding byte up, truncating to 40 bits at each shift, and
  a zero byte after a zero byte shifts nine places, not eight. A quotient is
  34 bits from its integer bit, over the divisor rounded to 32.
- **Decimal.** Input multiplies by ten and adds each digit, then scales by
  tens. STR$ and PRINT take the unrounded result: below 1 a product by 1E9
  first, then by ten while the rounded value is under 99999999.9375 and by a
  tenth while over 999999999.25, then a half added and nine digits taken.
- **Functions.** SIN divides by 2π, folds the turn's fraction to a quarter
  and evaluates six odd coefficients; COS adds π/2 first; TAN divides SIN by
  the cosine of SIN's folded argument. ATN takes the reciprocal above 1 and
  evaluates twelve. LOG evaluates four on (m−1/√2)/(m+1/√2) of the
  mantissa. EXP evaluates eight on the fraction of x·log₂e, taken with 80 in
  its rounding byte, and is zero below 2⁻¹²⁷. SQR raises its rounded
  argument to a half. RND multiplies by 11879546, adds 3.927677739E-8 and
  reverses the mantissa's bytes over the old exponent; a negative argument
  seeds with its own bytes reversed, zero with timer A and the time of day.

## 6. The front end and verification

The model joins the library as BEEB §5's do. Its assistant asks for the
standard, the SID and the drives, and its settings change them and the four
sockets (KERNAL, BASIC, characters, each drive's DOS) only while the machine
is off, the keypad joystick's port at any time. Keys map to the C64's layout
by character and by position, the host's Alt the Commodore key, Escape
RUN/STOP and F12 or Page Up RESTORE, and the numeric keypad drives the stick
in the port chosen; the status strip lights the datasette's motor and each
1541. Disks, tapes, cartridges and programs come from the picker, a drop and
Open With, disks and tapes from the machine's folder too, where *New Disk*
formats a D64 and *New Tape* makes a TAP. Paste types each letter as the
machine's character set shows it: each slice reports its letter case, the
upper-case set typing a small letter as its capital.

Conformance follows BEEB §7. The corpus in `tests/corpus/c64/` — BASIC typed
at its prompt, the screen editor, and the DOS through BASIC on a freshly
formatted disk — is recorded by `cargo xtask openmicro --reference` from
KERNAL 3, BASIC V2 and 1541 DOS 2.6 held as `c64-kernal.rom`,
`c64-basic.rom`, `c64-character.rom` and `c1541-dos.rom`, and replayed
against the clean-room ROMs by `tests/c64_corpus.rs`. Each device is tested
against its published timing with programs written for it, and `fuzz_media`,
`fuzz_snapshot` and `fuzz_worker` cover every C64 medium, its snapshots under
hostile guest code, and its protocol.
